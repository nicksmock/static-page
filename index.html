Two things I automated
because no one else was going to.

I'm Nick Smock, and I'm sole IT person at my company. Both of these came out of the same problem: two lists that nobody was comparing. I built them using Claude Code.

Access control

Finding the people who left without telling IT

Problem
As the only IT person, I find out someone left when somebody tells me. That notification often didn't arrive, so accounts stayed active after the person was gone. I couldn't fix the handoff, so I stopped depending on it.
Built
A scheduled PowerShell job that pulls the current directory user list, compares it against the employee roster from HR, and flags accounts that don't reconcile for review and disablement.
Result
Departures are detected from HR's own records instead of a handoff that may never happen. The first run surfaced active accounts belonging to people who no longer worked there.
DirectoryHR roster
j.okafor=j.okafor
m.castellanos=m.castellanos
r.whitfield!no match
t.nguyen=t.nguyen

Anything without a match is a person who left and an account that didn't. Illustrative names.

Security awareness

One report instead of two spreadsheets

Problem
Training completion and phishing simulation results come out of our security awareness platform as separate exports. The people who matter most are the ones in both, who failed a simulation and skipped the training, and finding them meant cross-referencing by hand every week.
Built
A parser that reads both exports and produces one readable report, sorted so the highest-risk users are at the top.
Result
About an hour a week back, so roughly fifty hours a year. The bigger change is that the report gets read now, because it's one page instead of two spreadsheets.

How I work with it

Claude writes most of the PowerShell I deploy. I read it, test it, and stage it before it touches anything in production, which matters more than usual when the script disables accounts for a living. I also use it to build internal tools, write end-user runbooks, and produce staff training, including a company-wide briefing on AI data privacy for a workforce that is not especially technical. That last part is most of the job. Automation is easy compared to getting people to use it.
